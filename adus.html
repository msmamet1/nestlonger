Is an ADU right for you

More family time, less institutional care.

ADUs aren’t right for every family or every lot. They’re the right move when proximity matters more than the perfect home, and when you’d rather invest in a permanent solution than a recurring monthly bill.

A second front door, not a wing on yours.

The right ADU gives your parent privacy, autonomy, and dignity, their own kitchen, their own routines, their own friends visiting at the door. You’re across the yard, not across the country.

Done well, it’s also the most flexible asset on the property: caregiver suite today, rental income later, adult-child landing pad after that.

Common questions

What families ask us first.

How can families finance an ADU?

Four common financing paths: cash-out refinance (cheapest with strong equity), HELOC (flexible draw, interest-only during construction), renovation loans like Fannie Mae HomeStyle and FHA 203(k), and state ADU programs in California, Oregon, and a growing list of states.

Will an ADU increase my property value?

Published estimates vary widely, from roughly 7% to over 40%, and nearly all of the research comes from California and Oregon markets with acute housing shortages. There is no reliable Massachusetts figure. The dependable returns are rental income and the option of housing a parent nearby, not a specific percentage lift.

Are ADUs allowed everywhere?

Not everywhere, but in Massachusetts the statewide rule is settled. Since 2 February 2025, one accessory dwelling unit of up to 900 square feet, or half the primary home’s floor area if that is smaller, can be built by right in any single-family zone, with no special permit. Local zoning still governs details like setbacks and parking, so NestLonger checks what’s allowed on your specific lot before matching you with a contractor.
