Why it matters

A fall changes everything. A grab bar changes the odds.

One in four older adults falls each year (CDC, National Council on Aging). The fix is unglamorous, cheap, and proven. The hard part is getting it done before something happens.

The fix is boring. The math is not.

A bar only helps if it is anchored into structure and placed where the transfer actually happens. Plan on two, one inside the shower, one by the toilet.

The work takes two hours. The hard part is finding someone who anchors into the framing instead of the drywall, and who shows up on time. That’s our job.

Insurance coverage

Find out what your parent’s plan actually covers.

Coverage for home safety modifications depends on the specific plan, the state, and the year. Here are the three places worth checking, and we check them with you before anyone spends money.

Medicare Advantage

Whether a plan includes home safety modifications as a supplemental benefit varies by plan and by year. We check your parent’s specific plan.

VA Benefits

The VA’s HISA grant can help eligible veterans with home modifications. It is capped and requires approval from a VA physician. We help you find the current limits and whether your parent qualifies. Details at va.gov.

Long-term care insurance

Whether a policy reimburses home safety improvements, and what it requires, varies widely between policies. We help you read yours and submit.

Common questions

What families ask us first.

How effective are grab bars at preventing falls?

Reducing hazards in the home, including installing grab bars, cuts the overall rate of falls among older adults by about 26%, according to a 2023 Cochrane systematic review of 22 studies covering 8,463 people (PubMed 36893804). The review measures home hazard reduction as a whole rather than grab bars alone. What matters in practice is anchoring into wall framing rather than drywall, and placing bars where the user will actually reach.
